The administrative short-sale treatment applies to **all open cases**, under
Rn. 324; the general Abgeltungsteuer regime starts with VZ 2009. It consequently
reaches the ordinary securities sales in VZ 2021–2025. Rn. 196 is not one of the
paragraphs for which Rn. 325 permits the earlier wording for withholding before
2026. This is not a new rule first applying in VZ 2025. The circular's date is
14.05.2025, not 19.04.2025.

Scope: ordinary short sales of securities held in private assets. A short
derivative exposure is not, merely because it is short, a sale of borrowed
securities. Stillhalterpraemien have their own rule, [GT-ESTG20-004]. Fund-unit
classification and partial exemption require the InvStG references as well;
the ordinary-share rules below do not establish their treatment by analogy.

## [GT-ESTG20-071] Original sale and attribution of actual covering costs

Rn. 196, opening condition and first operative sentence:

> Verfügt der Kunde über keinen Bestand und erteilt er einen Verkaufsauftrag,
> gilt Folgendes: Der Verkaufsauftrag muss sofort als Veräußerungsgeschäft
> abgewickelt werden.

Rn. 196, following paragraph, sentences 1 and 3:

> Deckt der Kunde sich anschließend mit entsprechenden Wertpapieren ein, hat
> das Kreditinstitut die hierfür angefallenen Anschaffungskosten nachträglich
> dem Veräußerungsgeschäft zuzuordnen.
>
> Das Erfüllungsgeschäft (Lieferung der Wertpapiere) ist steuerlich nicht relevant.

The intervening sentence requires correction of withholding from the substitute
basis to the **actual disposal gain including actual acquisition costs**. The
next paragraph expressly includes covering in a later calendar year; its final
sentence permits the customer to attribute the covering purchase to the preceding
sale in assessment under § 32d Abs. 4. The paragraph also prescribes a deemed
paid depot transfer at market price when the bank cannot make that attribution.
That is the bank's withholding procedure, not a second economic sale to be
added to the customer's correctly attributed disposal result. The final
paragraph restates the bank's same-calendar-year correction duty.

For shares, the substantive disposal is § 20 Abs. 2 Satz 1 Nr. 1 and its gain
is computed under Abs. 4 Satz 1: proceeds less directly connected disposal
expenses and the attributable acquisition costs ([GT-ESTG20-011],
[GT-ESTG20-068]). For capital claims, Nr. 7 is the relevant classification.
Covering costs belong to that **original disposal**, not to a new disposal
gain arising merely because the purchase took place in a later year.

The original proceeds' receipt year follows § 11 Abs. 1 Satz 1. Rn. 85 instead
fixes the contract date for currency conversion and gain measurement
([GT-ESTG20-039]); that sentence must not be expanded into a universal rule
equating receipt with contract conclusion. Section 11 Abs. 1 also contains
exceptions for regularly recurring income, prepaid use consideration and wages,
and preserves business profit determination; none makes a covering purchase
the receipt of the earlier securities sale proceeds.

**Prior assessments:** attributing costs to the original sale does not itself
choose the procedural provision for reopening a particular assessment.
Section 175 Abs. 1 Satz 1 Nr. 2 requires an event having retrospective tax
effect; Satz 2 regulates the limitation period. Abs. 2 Satz 2 expressly says
later presentation of a certificate is not itself such an event. Rn. 196 does
not cite § 175 AO. A specific amendment route requires the facts of the original
assessment and when the covering purchase occurred; it is not established by
quoting § 175 alone.

Rn. 197 separately addresses a merely technical negative holding caused by
booking the purchase after the sale despite the opposite contractual order.
Such a booking sequence must not be mistaken for an actual short sale.

## [GT-ESTG20-072] The substitute basis belongs to withholding

Section 43a Abs. 2 Satz 7 says:

> Sind die Anschaffungsdaten nicht nachgewiesen, bemisst sich der Steuerabzug
> nach 30 Prozent der Einnahmen aus der Veräußerung oder Einlösung der Wirtschaftsgüter.

This is **30% of disposal receipts as the withholding base**, not a 30% tax
rate and not the security's year-end position value. The withholding rate is
separately prescribed in § 43a Abs. 1 Satz 1 Nr. 1. Rn. 196's first paragraph,
second sentence explicitly invokes this provision for a short sale without
acquisition costs. No year-end mark-to-market calculation is prescribed there.

The surrounding § 43a Abs. 2 also regulates documented costs, depot transfers,
substitute market prices and special withholding cases. It does not turn
Satz 7 into a general assessment formula. Section 32d Abs. 3 Satz 1 requires
declaration of taxable capital income that did not bear German withholding.
Abs. 4 instead provides review for income that **did** bear withholding;
Rn. 194 requires supporting documents for that correction.

Consequently, Rn. 196 / § 43a Abs. 2 Satz 7 alone does **not establish** a
mandatory 30% declaration basis for an uncovered sale through a foreign broker
without German withholding. It also does not authorize omission of the sale.

## [GT-ESTG20-073] Still uncovered at assessment: unresolved quantification

For a sale still uncovered when the declaration is prepared, actual future
acquisition costs do not yet exist. The authorities above establish the original
sale and the later attribution, but do not supply a foreign-broker-specific
fixed provisional assessment amount. The unresolved issue is **quantification
before covering**, not permission to tax all completed sales in the cover year.

Section 162 Abs. 1 Saetze 1–2 requires the tax authority to estimate bases it
cannot determine or calculate, taking all relevant circumstances into account.
Its other paragraphs address deficient cooperation/records, foreign-related
documentation, surcharges and preliminary assessment of separately determined
bases; none prescribes a short-sale percentage.

See **Q22** for the candidate readings and their limits. No authoritative
support located in this review establishes either a universal 30% estimate or
an automatic zero-cost/full-proceeds assessment for this case. A concrete
provisional assessment basis cannot be called settled law on this evidence.

## [GT-ESTG20-074] Full factual disclosure of a deviating assessment position

Sources retrieved/read 2026-09-22:

- **Tier 1:** [§ 90 Abs. 1 Saetze 1–3 AO](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/ao_1977/__90.html):
  cooperation, complete and truthful disclosure of material facts and known
  evidence; the extent depends on the individual circumstances. Abs. 2 adds
  duties for foreign facts; Abs. 3 concerns cross-border business documentation.
- **Tier 1:** [§ 150 Abs. 2 and Abs. 7 Satz 1 AO](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/ao_1977/__150.html):
  truthful declarations to the best of one's knowledge; a field for information
  requiring assessment by an official. Abs. 7 Satz 2 separately concerns eDaten.
- **Tier 1:** [§ 370 Abs. 1 Nr. 1–2 AO](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/ao_1977/__370.html):
  incorrect/incomplete factual information or wrongful failure to disclose,
  causing a tax reduction or unjustified advantage. Abs. 4 Satz 1 includes
  delayed assessment; an expectation of the same eventual tax does not excuse
  incorrect disclosure. [§ 378 Abs. 1 AO](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/ao_1977/__378.html)
  separately addresses grossly negligent conduct.
- **Tier 4:** BGH **10.11.1999, 5 StR 221/99**, reasons **Rn. 22–25**,
  [full-text reproduction](https://www.hrr-strafrecht.de/5/99/5-221-99.php).
  Rn. 22: even a favourable incorrect legal view is not incorrect information
  under § 370 Abs. 1 Nr. 1 when all tax-relevant facts are correctly and fully
  stated, enabling correct assessment under a different legal interpretation.
  Rn. 24–25 addresses numerical declarations and requires disclosure of facts
  whose relevance is doubtful, especially where the view differs from case
  law, administrative guidance or usual assessment practice.

This supports submitting a **disclosed legal contention**, not an entitlement
to its acceptance. An assertion of zero income pending covering must not be
presented as zero sale receipts: the received proceeds, allocated expenses,
quantities, dates, partial covers and resulting numerical exclusions must be
apparent. A generic disclaimer or an unlabeled holdings table is insufficient.
Known covers and their treatment must also be disclosed; a year-end inventory
alone makes no representation about whether covering occurred after year-end.

For the contention that the net short-sale result should enter the return only
on covering, the argument is the actual-gain calculation under § 20 Abs. 4
Satz 1 and the absence of a direct foreign-assessment substitute in § 43a Abs. 2
Satz 7. The contrary receipt-year/original-disposal reasoning in [GT-ESTG20-071]
remains material and must not be described as refuted. Neither the BGH decision
nor § 150 AO decides short-sale timing or makes an interim zero a statutory
assessment base. Subsequent original-year adjustments depend on their own
procedural conditions; § 175 AO is not an unconditional promise of amendment.

Applicable to the disclosure of the VZ 2023–2025 cases discussed here. The
assessment-year form mappings remain those in the separate forms references.
The general duty in §§ 90/150 and the cited BGH interpretation predate these
years; the electronic human-review facility in § 150 Abs. 7 Satz 1 was introduced
by the Gesetz zur Modernisierung des Besteuerungsverfahrens, 18.07.2016,
BGBl. I S. 1679, effective 01.01.2017. This passage creates no tax exemption,
withholding rate or substantive commencement date.
